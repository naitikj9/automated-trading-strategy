# Trader's playbook

How to think about a stock, the market, and risk. These are principles, not rules; explain the reasoning when you use them.

## 1. Read the market before the stock

Most stocks follow the market and their sector. Check, in order:

1. **Nifty regime.** Up: close and EMA 50 above EMA 200. Down: both below. Chop: in between. In a down regime, breakouts fail more and rallies get sold.
2. **India VIX.** Below 12 is calm, and trends run (the strategy's best trades came here). 12–20 is normal. Above 20 is fear: wide swings, gaps, and stops get hit.
3. **Breadth.** The share of index stocks above their EMA 50. Above 70% is a broad, healthy rally. Under 30% is washed out, where the next trend often starts. 30–70% is the mixed middle, where the strategy did worst.
4. **Sector strength.** Compare the sector index's return this year with Nifty. A stock in a leading sector has the wind behind it. Leadership rotates every year, so check it fresh rather than assuming.

## 2. Read the stock's chart

- **Trend:** where the close sits against EMA 20, 50 and 200, and whether EMA 20 is above EMA 50. Rising EMAs that are spread apart mean a healthy trend.
- **Strength:** +DI vs −DI shows who is in control. ADX shows how strong the trend is: 15–20 is a trend starting, 25+ is established, 35+ is very strong. A rising ADX is more important than its level.
- **Momentum:** MACD above its signal line, and above zero, confirms the move. A bearish divergence (price higher high, MACD lower high) is a warning, not an exit signal.
- **Levels:** the last swing high (resistance to break), the last swing low and EMA 20 (first support), and EMA 50 (the strategy's exit line).
- **Candle:** a close near the day's high means buyers held control into the close. A long upper wick after a big run means selling into strength.
- **Normal noise:** compute the average daily move over the last 60 days. A move inside that range is noise and is not a signal.
- **Extension:** how far the close is above EMA 50. Being far above is not a reason to avoid momentum (the study showed extended entries did well), but it raises the odds of a sharp pullback, so expect the swings to be bigger.

## 3. Forming a view

- Say what would prove you wrong before saying what you expect.
- Give both cases, then a lean with a confidence level. For tomorrow, an honest confidence is rarely above 55–60%. Over weeks, trend and regime give a real edge.
- Price targets are guesses; levels are useful. Say "above ₹350 opens room toward the next high; a close under ₹324 (EMA 20) weakens it" rather than "it will go to ₹380".
- News, results dates, and index rebalances can override any chart. Ask about or mention them when they could matter.

## 4. Risk and sizing

- **Risk per trade, not capital per trade.** With the strategy's exit at EMA 50, the real risk is the gap between entry and EMA 50 (at least 2%, often 5–15%). A ₹1 lakh slot entered 13% above EMA 50 risks about ₹13,000 if the trend breaks.
- **Expect the drawdown.** The 10-slot setup has had −24% drawdowns. Real capital should be sized so a −25% drop is survivable financially and emotionally.
- **Correlation.** Ten slots in one sector are really one bet. Spread across sectors.
- **Losing streaks are normal.** With a 30% win rate, runs of 8–10 losers in a row happen. That is when systems get abandoned right before they pay.
- **Never average down** in a trend-following system. The exit exists so a broken trend costs a small loss, not a big one.

## 5. Trader psychology

- The urge to book a quick profit is what kills trend following. The money is in the few trades held for months.
- After a big up day, the best entry has often already passed. Whoever buys the close takes on the next day's noise.
- Being flat is a position. In chop, a system that sits out is doing its job.
- Process over outcome: a well-executed losing trade is a good trade; a lucky win that broke the rules is a bad one.
